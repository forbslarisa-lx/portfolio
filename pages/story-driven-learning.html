Featured Learning Experience

Meet Pedro

Pedro has spent two years studying English. In class, he participates, communicates, and improves. Outside the classroom, however, his work, income, and future remain unchanged. The story briefly shows the future he could have reached through a CDL pathway, then reveals that he never learned the program existed, left class, and never returned.

Open full screen
Scenario

The animation deliberately tells two stories: the future Pedro could have achieved and the future he actually experienced. The difference between them is not motivation. It is visibility. The story reframes learner attrition as a question of institutional awareness, communication, and responsibility.

Pedro did not fail to find the pathway. We failed to make the pathway visible.

Before asking why learners leave, we should first ask what we did to help them see a meaningful next step.

Business Need

Every invisible pathway has a cost

Opportunities cannot change lives when learners never know they exist.

01

Business challenge

Adult Education programs invest substantial effort in workforce pathways, integrated education, and career training. Yet those programs create no impact when learners never hear about them, never understand how they connect to their goals, or never realize they exist.

When pathways remain invisible, learners may conclude that English classes have no connection to the future they hoped to build. Every invisible opportunity carries a retention cost and a missed chance for economic mobility.

02

Project goal

Create a short staff learning experience that interrupts the assumption that learner attrition reflects motivation alone and encourages employees to examine the institution's role in communicating opportunity, relevance, and meaningful next steps.

The experience needed to be emotionally resonant, brief enough to hold attention, and strong enough to provoke reflection before directing staff toward deeper learning.

03

The solution

Instead of presenting statistics or procedural guidance, the learning experience uses narrative reversal and counterfactual storytelling.

Viewers first see the future Pedro could have achieved through a CDL pathway. Only afterward do they learn that the ending never happened because Pedro was never introduced to the opportunity. The reversal turns a success story into a question of institutional responsibility.

Learning Design Decisions

Designed to interrupt the familiar story

Each design choice was intended to create reflection, emotional contrast, and a shift from learner-blame toward systems thinking.

01

Narrative reversal

The story allows viewers to believe they are watching a familiar learner success narrative before revealing that the successful ending never occurred.

02

Counterfactual storytelling

Showing what could have happened makes the institutional gap more visible and emotionally tangible than simply describing a missed referral.

03

Reflection before instruction

The experience raises questions before offering answers, increasing the likelihood that staff will examine their own assumptions and practice.

04

Systems thinking

Pedro is never positioned as the sole cause of the outcome. Attention shifts toward communication, visibility, retention, and organizational responsibility.

Intended Performance Impact

Visible pathways create meaningful reasons to persist

This portfolio prototype demonstrates the intended performance impact of the intervention. Formal implementation and evaluation data have not yet been collected.

Greater pathway visibility

Staff become more intentional about introducing opportunities learners may not know exist.

Stronger learner retention

Learners better understand how today's classes connect to tomorrow's career and economic goals.

More purposeful staff conversations

Employees more consistently connect learner aspirations with available education and workforce pathways.

Institutional reflection

Teams examine how organizational communication, staff awareness, and everyday practice influence learner persistence and success.

Reflection

Sometimes the most important learning outcome is changing the question.

This project was never about teaching staff how to describe a CDL program. It was about challenging one of the most familiar explanations for learner attrition.

Pedro's imagined future demonstrates the value of the pathway. His actual outcome reveals the cost of keeping that pathway invisible. Instead of asking, “Why did Pedro leave?” the experience asks, “What did we do to help him stay?”
